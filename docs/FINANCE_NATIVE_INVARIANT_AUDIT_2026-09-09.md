## Inventory and evidence boundary

An AST scan of every first-party `*/models.py` found **21 conditional UniqueConstraint declarations**: 15 in Accounting, Finance, vouchers and reporting, and six in supporting modules. All 21 correspond to `models.W036` warnings in the final v0.7.70 MySQL run. Two additional warnings belong to third-party `account.EmailAddress`, giving 23 warnings overall. The accompanying [machine-readable inventory](finance-discovery/NATIVE_CONDITIONAL_IDENTITIES_2026-09-09.json) records each model, key, predicate, source line and database alias.

The scan covers declarations in the current checkout, not every possible application invariant. It does not inventory missing constraints, ordinary unique keys, check constraints, external integrations or dynamically generated models. Third-party code is not copied or patched. Budget and active bank-match identities repaired in v0.7.69–70 are ordinary generated-key constraints and therefore do not appear here.

The [database router](../src/database_routers.py) routes Accounting and Budget to `finance`; Finance setup, vouchers, reporting and the supporting modules use `default`. Both stores were MySQL in the cited regression. Keep this distinction when choosing transaction boundaries: a default-store Department lock cannot serialize a Finance-store transaction.

Source inspection shows controls and plausible race boundaries; it is **not a successful concurrent reproduction**. A missing native constraint is an enforcement gap, not evidence that a supported workflow has created duplicate financial records. Do not declare a finding verified or accepted merely because sequential tests pass or a lock appears in code.
